# Staged xk6 continuity qualification plan

Revision 0.1, 2026-11-24. **Preparation only: no test, Job creation, sync, fault injection or 24-hour run is authorized by this plan.** User instruction is to qualify continuous production/consumption cheaply with **100 MB total original producer value payload over 24 hours**, then finalize and commit the corresponding **2 TB/24h and 20 TB/24h configurations after successful completion**. The large configurations are not generated or committed in this preparation. Neither later configuration publication nor a successful sandbox run automatically launches a large run.

## Accounting and what the test proves

Initial convention: decimal 100 MB=100,000,000 unique original value bytes, consistent with the existing preflight contract. Consumer reads, RF2 replication, keys/headers, protocol/TLS, retries, metrics and logs are additional traffic. This is not a 100 MB cap on all network/storage billing. Existing worker/node/PVC uptime can still incur charges; low payload volume does not guarantee low total cluster cost. This test demonstrates continuity and accounting under very low load, not production throughput, storage capacity, latency under load or crash durability.

Use four existing smoke topics and one persistent producer/consumer pair per topic. Proposed small values are **1,000 bytes**, raw seeded ASCII, no compression; **100,000 records total**, 25,000/topic, 25,000,000 value bytes/topic. Give each record a unique run/topic/sequence key and independently computed checksum. One fresh stable consumer group per topic, continuous polling; no deliberate consumer pauses, random schedules or injected faults in this continuity profile. Broader PF randomized schedules remain separate scenarios. Do not infer production application activity from this synthetic schedule.

Exact paced schedule: global record index i=0..99,999, due at `(i+0.5)*86400/100000` seconds after measurement t0; topic=i modulo 4. First due 0.432s, last due 86,399.568s; aggregate interval 0.864s; per-topic interval 3.456s. The average is **1,157.407 original value bytes/s**, approximately 1.157 kB/s, and **1.157 records/s**. Bound lateness rather than accumulating an unlimited end-of-test burst. There are no application retries in the first candidate; a failed/ambiguous call is not silently resent or counted as acknowledged. Native retries remain bounded by the selected delivery timeout and are additional traffic.

The 24-hour **measurement/generation interval** is 86,400 seconds. Separately allow setup up to 120s, drain up to 300s and shutdown up to 60s: **hard total Job envelope 86,880s (24h 8m)**. Consumers must be assigned before t0 and remain active through generation and the bounded drain. A timer keeping a sleeping process alive is not evidence of continuity; each topic/group needs ongoing timestamped acknowledgements/receipts and heartbeats. The old five-minute boundary-smoke limit applies only to that earlier test.

## Acceptance, monitoring and stop policy

Candidate continuity bounds to validate in S2/S3: heartbeat every 60s; each topic's last successful acknowledgement and each group's last verified receipt no older than 60s during the measured phase; generation start lateness at most5s; verified lag no older than 60s. These are proposed synthetic-test thresholds, not production SLAs. Include client timestamps as well as broker consumer lag—zero lag can coexist with a stalled producer. Compare schedule and record counts in every minute and check both the start and end of the full interval.

For S4 PASS require exactly 100,000 unique acknowledged originals /100,000,000 value bytes, all expected IDs received by their groups, checksum/size/topic/group correctness, no missing/unresolved outcomes, no duplicates for this no-application-retry candidate, no unexplained timing gaps, and no new unexpected worker/broker restarts. Initial restart counts must be captured; existing broker counts are not zero by assumption. Track expected versus attempted versus acknowledged versus received bytes separately. Classify planned, late, unknown and failed outcomes; do not turn a shortfall into a pass by extending generation. All expected receipts must reconcile before the 300s drain expires. Missing raw events/heartbeats or unproven timer bounds make the result INCONCLUSIVE/INCOMPLETE, even if a final counter claims success.

Collect existing observability from **both kaas-kafka and kaas-demo**: worker/broker CPU, memory/RSS/heap, throttling, restarts/OOM, PVC usage and storage latency, Kafka ingress/egress/errors, ISR/under-replication, consumer lag and client last-success/gaps. Availability of observability was user-reported; concrete metric/export coverage still needs checking. Stop/fail on checksum mismatch, evidence loss, unavailability, deadline violation, unexpected restart, backlog or resource growth beyond reviewed bounds; record stop reason and incomplete totals. Do not alter quotas/storage/security during the run to hide a failure. No fault injection or duplicate infrastructure durability campaign is required for this low-load continuity stage.

Use an **external durable event collector/ledger throughout the day**, not one final Pod-log download (the old helper's bounded log capture would truncate this run). Export IDs, hashes, phase/outcome, timings and locations when exposed; never values, passwords or complete connection configs. At 100k records, four 500-byte record events (attempt, ACK, receipt, commit) alone would be roughly 200 MB—an illustrative estimate, not measured volume. Initially reserve at least 1 GiB for evidence, validate actual event size/rotation/collector throughput in S2/S3, and stop if the collector cannot persist evidence. Keep reconciliation ledger state beyond six-hour Kafka retention and past run completion. Polling the console or one helper process staying alive is insufficient collection assurance.

Six-hour retention stays selected. Reconcile continuously; early records may have expired by hour 24, so replaying everything only at the end cannot prove delivery. Time-based deletion is segment-based and not an exact per-record expiry deadline. A uniform-input RF2 six-hour retained-value reference is about 50 MB for this small run, excluding segment/index/metadata, storage redundancy and headroom. See [Kafka 4.2 topic configuration](https://kafka.apache.org/42/configuration/topic-configs/) for retention/segment and message-limit semantics; review against effective runtime settings before execution. Sandbox lifetime, owner availability, log/evidence capacity, current credential/CA validity, temporary Secret cleanup and emergency stop control must be recorded before starting the day-long test.

A100 MB PASS establishes the harness can remain active and reconcile low-rate traffic for a day. It does **not** establish that this three-broker/60 Gi dev deployment can ingest terabytes. Preserve the successful configuration and evidence as immutable input to S5; only then generate/finalize and commit two disabled24h volume profiles:

| WP-B | 20,000,000,000,000 bytes | 231.481481 MB/s | 10,000,000,000,000 bytes |

These alternatives are not additive and are not production peak estimates. Recompute per-topic budgets, record counts from explicitly selected payload distributions, worker pacing/concurrency, evidence strategy, stop bounds and resource/storage requirements. Preserve WP-A topic sizes 1k–10M and WP-B 10k–300K with the declared initial decimal conventions. Do not carry the continuity test's fixed 1,000-byte values into WP-B and call it representative. WP-A's10,000,000-byte upper case exceeds the current 3MiB transport/2MiB-qualified envelope; decide and validate its limits through GitOps and a separately authorized boundary check before exercising it. No claim check/offloading is introduced implicitly.

The uniform RF2/6h references of 1 TB/10 TB, even before overhead, exceed the dev cluster's60 Gi allocated claims. Publishing disabled volume profiles is not approval to run them on this sandbox. Select a suitable target/retention/resource envelope before later full-scale execution. Keep the no-loss objective and inherited infrastructure persistence dependency; continuity or 6h retention alone does not certify recovery/no-loss behavior.
